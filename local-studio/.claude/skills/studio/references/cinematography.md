# Cinematography cheat sheet (for prompts and shot lists)

Pick choices on purpose. Every shot should have a reason: what the viewer should feel or notice.

## Shot sizes
| Size | Use |
|---|---|
| Extreme wide / establishing | Place, scale, context (city, building exterior) |
| Wide / full | Subject in environment, body language |
| Medium / waist | Conversation, presenting, most "talking" content |
| Medium close-up (chest up) | Warm, personal, trustworthy |
| Close-up (face) | Emotion, decision moments |
| Extreme close-up / macro | Detail: signature on a document, pen nib, eyes |
| Insert | Hands, phone screen, product — breaks up talking shots |

## Angles
- **Eye level**: neutral, honest (default for business/trust).
- **Low angle**: power, confidence, aspiration (founder walking toward camera).
- **High angle**: vulnerability, overview; top-down (flat lay) for desks/documents.
- **Dutch tilt**: tension, unease — rarely right for premium product work.
- **Over-the-shoulder**: puts viewer in the conversation.
- **POV**: immersion ("you" opening the approval email).

## Lenses (full-frame equivalent)
| Focal length | Look |
|---|---|
| 14–24mm | Expansive, dramatic perspective, real estate interiors (keep verticals straight) |
| 35mm | Natural, documentary, environment + subject |
| 50mm | Human-eye neutral |
| 85mm | Classic portrait, flattering compression, creamy bokeh |
| 100–135mm | Tight portraits, compressed backgrounds, isolation |
| Macro 100mm | Tiny detail, product |
| Anamorphic (40/50mm) | Cinematic widescreen, oval bokeh, horizontal flares |

Aperture words: f/1.4–f/2 = very shallow depth of field; f/4–f/5.6 = balanced; f/8–f/11 = deep focus (architecture, groups).

## Camera movement (video)
One move per clip. Name it precisely and give it a speed.
- **Static / locked-off** — calm, lets action play.
- **Slow push-in (dolly-in)** — builds importance/intimacy.
- **Pull-out (dolly-out)** — reveal context.
- **Truck / lateral track** — follows movement, shows space.
- **Pan / tilt** — reveal across or up (tilt up a building = aspiration).
- **Orbit / arc** — hero moment around a subject or product.
- **Crane / jib up** — grand reveal, ending shot.
- **Handheld (subtle)** — documentary realism, urgency.
- **Drone flyover / top-down** — property and location establishing.
- **Rack focus** — shift attention foreground → background.

## Lighting setups
- **Soft window light** — natural, trustworthy, editorial.
- **Golden hour** — warm, optimistic, aspirational; long shadows.
- **Blue hour** — calm, premium, city lights on.
- **Rembrandt / low-key** — dramatic, serious, one side lit.
- **High-key** — bright, clean, minimal shadow (tech/finance clean look).
- **Rim / backlight** — separates subject from background, hero feel.
- **Practical lights** — lamps, screens, neon in frame for realism at night.
- **Overcast** — even, soft, great for skin.
Say where the light comes from ("key light from camera left through a window").

## Composition
Rule of thirds; leading lines (roads, hallways, table edges); negative space for text overlays (important for
ads — leave room on one side); symmetry for premium/architectural; foreground framing (doorways, plants,
glass reflections) for depth; eye-line room in the direction the subject looks.

## Looks & grades
- "Clean corporate": high-key, neutral whites, soft contrast.
- "Cinematic": teal-and-orange, lifted blacks, subtle grain, anamorphic.
- "Editorial": natural color, soft contrast, 35mm film grain (Portra 400 look).
- "Luxury": deep shadows, warm highlights, controlled reflections, slow movement.
- "Documentary": handheld feel, available light, candid, slightly desaturated.

## Objects & materials (the main focus)
Light the material, not just the object. Each material has a signature that sells realism:
| Material | Light it with | What must be visible |
|---|---|---|
| Brushed/polished metal | Long strip softboxes for clean gradient reflections; thin rim light on edges | Directional brushed grain, crisp specular edges, believable environment reflections |
| Chrome / mirror finish | Large controlled reflectors, dark surroundings (it reflects everything) | Clean highlight shapes, no random blobs, horizon line in reflections |
| Glass & liquids | Backlight or edge light through a diffuser; black or white card for edge definition | Refraction, caustics on the surface, bright/dark edge lines, condensation, bubbles |
| Wood | Raking side light at a low angle | Grain, pores, sanding marks, oil sheen |
| Leather / fabric | Soft window light at 45° | Pebbling, creases, stitching, weave, fuzz on edges |
| Ceramic / stone | Soft overcast or large diffused key | Glaze speckle, matte vs gloss contrast, chips, pitting |
| Food & organic | Backlight plus a fill card | Translucency (citrus, leaves), moisture, crumbs, imperfection |

Product angles:
- **3/4 hero** (camera slightly above, 30–45° off front): the default "catalog" shot.
- **Low-angle hero**: makes small objects monumental; add a rim light and haze.
- **Top-down flat lay**: arrangements, packaging, kits; keep shadows soft and consistent.
- **Macro detail**: texture, engraving, stitching, the "proof of quality" shot.
- **Profile / silhouette**: shape and design lines, strong backlight.
- **In-context environment**: the object where it lives (workbench, marble counter, desk).

Surfaces and sets: slate, concrete, travertine, marble, raw linen, end-grain wood, seamless paper, wet black
stone, acrylic risers. Name the surface; it anchors scale and realism.

Object motion (video):
- **Turntable / orbit**: slow 360° or 90° arc around a centered product.
- **Slow push-in**: from 3/4 hero to macro detail.
- **Macro slide**: lateral track across a texture or engraving at very shallow depth of field.
- **Light sweep**: static camera, a highlight travels across the surface (great for metal, glass, embossing).
- **Reveal**: object rises into frame, lid opens, cloth slides off.
- **Pour / splash / drop**: liquids and particles, often slow-motion.
- **Rack focus**: foreground texture to the hero object.
Keep one move per clip, slow and steady; the object itself should not deform.

## Realism boosters (stills)
Mention skin texture/pores, natural imperfections, real fabric wrinkles, specular highlights, depth of field,
lens characteristics, film grain, correct shadows matching the light source, believable wardrobe and props.

## Continuity across shots
Keep a "bible" in the shot list defaults: same wardrobe description, same location description, same time of
day and light direction, same grade words. Reuse the same seed family for a character when not using a LoRA.
